Garden · late summer

Tomatoes, grown badly on purpose.

Compost, patience, and a watering can. No growth hacks. The soil here is clay and the tomatoes are small, but they taste like tomatoes.

The first year I tried to do this properly. I read about soil blocks, fish emulsion, and the correct way to prune a determinate variety. I bought a moisture meter. The plants were fine. I was not.

The second year I did almost nothing. I turned kitchen scraps into a pile, waited until it stopped smelling like a mistake, and dug it into the clay. I planted too late. I watered when the leaves looked sad. The tomatoes were smaller and better.

The garden is not a productivity system. It is a place where the feedback loop is measured in weeks. That is the whole reason to keep one.

What worked

Four things, none of them clever.

ThingWhy
CompostFree, slow, and it fixes clay instead of covering it.
MulchLess watering, fewer weeds, cooler roots.
Deep wateringOnce a week, properly, beats a daily sprinkle.
Leaving it aloneMost interventions were a way to feel useful.
