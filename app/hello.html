Note · from a laptop

This page is a folder on somebody's PC.

No server. No CDN. No build step. Just a drive, a key, and a port that isn't even open.

Somewhere, a laptop is sitting on a desk with a folder called app in it. That folder contains a handful of HTML files and one stylesheet. It also contains a keypair. The public half of that keypair is the address you typed to get here.

The gateway you are reading through asked the DHT who has that address. A peer answered. Blocks were exchanged, signatures were checked, and the HTML arrived. You did not connect to a data center. You connected to a computer that could be switched off at any moment.

That is the entire uptime model. There is no failover, no replica, and no status page. If the machine sleeps, the site sleeps. A web that can go offline is a web that belongs to somebody.

What just happened

Four small steps.

  1. You opened a key. The address decoded back into 32 bytes.
  2. The gateway joined a topic. That topic is the drive's discovery key.
  3. A peer answered. Hole punching, or a relay that sees ciphertext only.
  4. You read a signed file. A tampered drive fails to load.
