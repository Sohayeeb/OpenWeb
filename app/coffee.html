Coffee, one cup at a time.

A grinder, a kettle, and four minutes. Roast the beans dark, grind them coarse, and stop buying equipment. The best cup is the one you make tomorrow morning without thinking about it.

I have owned a machine with a screen. It made a sound like a small aircraft and produced coffee that was fine. I have owned a press, a pour-over cone, a cloth filter, and a scale that connected to my phone. The coffee did not get better in proportion to the equipment.

What worked was boring: fresh beans, a consistent coarse grind, water just off the boil, and enough time for the grounds to settle. Four minutes. No app.

The recipe is not the point. The point is that a small ritual you can repeat without optimization is worth more than a better cup you need a manual for. 

1. Boil water. Let it sit for thirty seconds.
2. Grind 18 g of beans, coarse.
3. Pour enough water to wet the grounds. Wait forty seconds.
4. Pour the rest slowly. Stop when the bed goes flat.
5. Drink it before it gets cold.

Serves one. Scales linearly if you are patient, which is the entire recipe.
