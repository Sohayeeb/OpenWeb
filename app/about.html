About

A neighborhood with a gate.

OpenWeb is not trying to replace the World Wide Web. It is trying to keep a small part of it honest: pages made by people, served by those people, and found without an auction.

The modern web is mostly a delivery mechanism for advertising and surveillance. Even the good parts are wrapped in scripts that decide what you are allowed to read. OpenWeb removes the middle and keeps the page. HTML, CSS, images, and a signature. That is the format.

The one rule: content is published by a human who signs it with a key, and it appears in a directory only after a human has looked at it. Everything else is unregulated. There is no algorithm to please, because there is no feed.

The shape

Four layers, each small enough to read.

01 · Address

A key, not a name

ow1… is the public half of a keypair. Encoding is reversible, so resolution needs no registry and no DNS.

see it in a page → 02 · Drive

An append-only log

A site is a Hyperdrive: signed blocks of files. A tampered block fails to load, which is the entire integrity model.

publish one → 03 · Search

A crawler plus a file

There is no global index. You crawl the seeds you trust, write an index you own, and answer queries locally.

Limits

What this is not, yet.

A small web has small coverage. We would rather say that out loud than pretend a crawl is the whole world.

CoverageSeeds you choose, plus the links those pages contain.
UptimeThe author's machine. Sleep it and the site sleeps.
JavaScriptNone in v1. Static pages only, by design.
RankingBM25 over words. No synonyms, no fuzzy matching, no personalization.
Search indexOne local JSON file. Roughly 3.4 KB per page at 20,000 pages.
NamesPetnames live in signed directory entries, never in the protocol.

Why bother

Because a page can be finished.

The web used to have edges. You could reach the end of a site, close the tab, and be done. OpenWeb is an attempt to make that feeling structural instead of nostalgic: no infinite feed, no recommendation engine, no notification waiting to pull you back.

It will not win. It does not need to. It needs to exist, be small enough to understand, and belong to the people who use it.

A web that can go offline is a web that belongs to somebody.
