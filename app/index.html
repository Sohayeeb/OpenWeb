The browser is the internet.

OpenWeb is a small, peer-to-peer web. Every page here is a file on a real computer, signed with a key and served to you by its owner. There is no platform in the middle, no account to create, and no bill at the end of the month.

The index lives on this computer. No query leaves it, and nothing here is ranked by clicks.

How it works

Six steps, and one of them is yours.

There is no DNS, no certificate authority, and no port to open. An address is a public key. The key is the site. If the key is valid, the page is the page its author signed.

  1. Write a folder. HTML, CSS, images. Static files only.
  2. Run one command. openweb serve ./my-site.
  3. Get an address. ow1…, derived from your key.
  4. Share the key. Anyone can resolve it. No registry.
  5. Find peers. The DHT introduces you; signed blocks move.
  6. Stay online. Your laptop is the server. Uptime is you.
The web was supposed to be a place you visited, not a feed you were placed into. This is a small attempt to give that back.
