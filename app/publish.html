Publish

Your PC is the host. That is the whole plan.

A site is a folder. Serving it is one command. The address is a key that belongs to you, not a name rented from somebody else.

OpenWeb does not need a deployment pipeline because there is nowhere to deploy to. The files stay on your disk. The node announces the drive's key on a DHT and answers readers directly. When you stop the process, the site goes offline. When you start it again, the same address comes back.

Step one

Make a folder with an index.html.

my-site/
├── index.html
├── style.css
└── another-page.html

Static files only. No JavaScript runs in v1. Relative links and links that start with / both work.

Step two

Publish it.

node packages/cli/dist/index.js serve my-site

Or, after npm link, just openweb serve my-site. The state lives in .openweb/ and the keypair never leaves your machine. 

online

  address   ow1vkmfwikivezc27lqizjyoapya2bqhyh63d6hofbn6rdpemdtybdq
  preview   http://127.0.0.1:7777/
  published 3 file(s) from my-site

Step three

Open the address through a gateway.

node packages/cli/dist/index.js open <address>

The gateway resolves the key, joins the drive's topic, and speaks plain HTTP to your browser. It is the only bridge between the two worlds.

Step four

Let people find it.

There is no global index. A search engine on OpenWeb is a crawler plus an index file that one person owns. Seed it with your address: 

node packages/cli/dist/index.js crawl <address> --dir .openweb --max 200
node packages/cli/dist/index.js search "your words" --dir .openweb

Or point the gateway at it and use the search box in the browser bar: http://127.0.0.1:8080/search?q=your+words.

Rules of the road

Small, signed, and accountable.

No adsNothing to sell means nothing to optimize for.
No trackersThe gateway sets no cookies and logs no visitors.
No AIPublish what you wrote. A human reviews the directory.
No take-backsYour key is your name. Revocation is a signed tombstone.
